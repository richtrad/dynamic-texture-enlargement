\begin{abstract}

A simple and fast approach for synthesizing dynamic textures that realistically matches given color 
		texture appearance and the optic flow is presented. The method generalizes the previous work in 
		spatial area which works well for static textures to the dynamic textures domain. Our method  
		is based on overlapping tiling and subsequent minimum boundary cut. Optimal toroidal dynamic texture 
		patches are created in each spatial and dynamic dimension, where they are derived from the optical
		 flow of the modeled texture. The toroidal patches are created in the analytical step which is 
		 separated from the synthesis part. The method is extremely fast (both in the spatial as well as 
		 in the time dimension), works well for many types of natural dynamic textures, and it is very easy 
		 to implement.

\end{abstract}

	Texture enlargement is widely recognized to be important for computer graphic applications in many ways because whatever size of measured data is, it is always insufficient  (i.e., for rendering of a complex and large scenes). The amount of measured data can be extremely high, thus some  dynamic texture 
compression method   is inevitable. Dynamic texture editing  is another  useful  application which allows to synthetize alternative types of DT, recreating sequences for studying of relationship between model parametres and visual appearance of the scene, etc. 

	The texture synthesis goal is to create a texture  which is perceptually similar to the example texture -  synthesized texture must reproduce a given digitized texture image so that original and reproduced one is ideally visually indiscernible.  However modeling of a natural texture is a very challenging and difficult task, due to unlimited variety of possible surfaces, illumination and viewing conditions simultaneously with the strong discriminative functionality of the human visual system. Also observations-nuisance-factors like artifact of unwanted regularity must be avoided.

	In this article, we present a new approach to generate infinitely large output (in all dimension) from a significantly smaller amount of measured input data. This is done by sophisticated composition of several  triple toroidal patches, which can be seamlessly repeated in every direction. 
By this way we can create a significantly larger pattern with similar stochastic properties. Our algorithm can finds toroidal patches in the input texture such that after giving patches abreast the borders between patches are visually indiscernible (seamless). This intelligent sampling allows to retain a small enough representative data samples to represent the whole texture. It also allows the creation of the infinitely large seamless output by repeating sequence of these toroidal samples.

		Our method can be used to both 3D or 4D textures. The principles of creating spatial and temporal cuts are generally similar, but since temporal dimension has different properties and relation to visual perceptions we develop different method for spatial and temporal cuts driven by optical flow. Purpose of this can be easy seen from the spatial and temporal property of dynamic textures - human perceive differently detects errors in the space and time \cite{Edelman92visualperception} because both spatial dimensions have similar property, hence the temporal not\cite{Fahle81spatiotemporalinterpolation}. In other words the spatial dimension may be interchanged and texture still keeps it structure. The time dimension has (unless some special cases like 4D procedural texture of materials) different property and can not be swapped with another spatial coordinate.

	\begin{equation} \label{eq:temporal_cut_cost}
		\Psi^{t*}  =  min_{\Psi}\{  \sum_{\forall r_1,r_2}{\psi_{r,m}^{t*}}\}
	\end{equation}
	
	where $t$ is unknown temporal coordinate of the first of most similar frames, $m$ is distance between the most similar frames.
	
	\begin{equation} \label{eq:temporal_cut_cost2}
	\psi_{x,y,t,m}^t =  D^m ( Y_r^g , Y^g_{r+[0,0,m]})\\
	\end{equation}
	where $D$ denotes distance operation with metric $m$ between operands of $D$. Maximal change of t between  8-neighborhood of $Y_{r}$ and $ Y_{r_1+i,r_2+j,r_3}$ is $c_t$.

		\section{Results}

In the most of cases (except comparison with other textures) we used for testing of our approaches database dynamic textures DynTex\cite{dyntex}, with textures with spatial resolution of 720 x 576 pixels and varying length (from seconds to minutes).This database contains a large number of many types, including grass, water, animals, smoke and other natural or artificial scene. In synthesis step, the output is not depend on type of input texture, but our approach works better on textures containing sharp edges (trees, leaves). Time of analysis part varies from minutes to hour given by length an input data and size of patches (if patches are large, the time-cut part of algorithm takes more time), while the synthesis takes significantly shorter time.

On Figs.~\ref{fig:clouds2},~\ref{fig:grass},~\ref{fig:spark} and~\ref{fig:water2} below you can see the comparison of our results previously published by Kwatra, Schodel and Bar-Joseph. All textures are 4D, but have extremely small resolution and artifacts created by using of lossy format (which work like automatic blur of seams) instead of uncompressed format which we used for all other results. 

On Fig. ~\ref{fig:clouds2} is the \textsc{clouds} texture (resolution is 128x128x59 by horizontal, vertical and time axis respectively). This is example of the texture where our algorithm does not work well, the clouds are unique and texture is extremely small to find perfect toroidal patch and the edge is visible in some cases (unless some blending method is used). On Fig.~\ref{fig:grass} the example of \textsc{grass} texture (resolution is 224x114x59 by horizontal, vertical and time axis respectively) is presented. The texture of \textsc{sparkle} (resolution is 240x160x150 by horizontal, vertical and time axis respectively) on Fig. ~\ref{fig:spark} is easy to create spatial cuts, but difficult to create the temporal one (due to uniques of texture frames). \paragraph{Distortions}
	Typical problems in the synthesis of the dynamic texture is the perspective distortions or rotating of the view. The synthesis of these types of scenes is difficult. Typical manifestation of this problem is not perfect vertical and eye-evident seam. The similar effect is present if smooth color or illumination transition (typically the sky) is in texture as can be seen texture \textsc{smoke}. Of course there are many types of textures, which has different horizontally consistency and the horizontally cut in it has no real sense (blasting flames, flag, panoramic view) or it will need more patches to keep it (top part of flag, patches of fluttering flag and its below part).
	
\paragraph{Highly dynamic texture}
	Some DT changed its structure extremely from frame to frame. Typical examples are flames (see \textsc{fireA} and \textsc{fireB} on Fig. ~\ref{fig:fire}), where the single flash of fire disappear and raised during small number of frames or by faster than capturing frequency. In many cases this can be overcome by manually setting of large areas for finding spatial and temporal cut by set-of-cuts as can be seen on picture below. In some cases our algorithm in automatic setting fails if there are some continually moving parts (flames going up) and still parts (bottom of flame) in the texture. It can be seen that synthesized picture looks like very good result, but degrades how flames changed. It is interesting, that after some optically wrong frames, algorithm found new local optimum, change the cut plane drastically and again starts from better results (and ally repeats). Fortunately the "wrong frames" are often only small part of texture and can be found and pick out without disturbing consistency of the whole sequences.

		\section {Summary}
		
		We have demonstrated a new approach for 3D image and 4D dynamic texture synthesis based on toroidal tiles. From synthesized examples can be seen that our approach is working for many types of DT. We have demonstrated some problems and in major part we show how our approach can handle it. Our approach is simple, can create infinitely long textures in all dimension and what is the most important it has completely separated analysis and synthesis part so synthesis can be done very quickly and effectively. In future we can improve our algorithm by computing perspective translation to overcome this problem and by more types of texture patches with different shape and better options for editing.
